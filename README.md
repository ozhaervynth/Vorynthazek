# Vorynthazek

> The tower's roots reached the clouds. Its summit remained buried beneath a field of salt.

## The Field That Held a Sky

Vorynthazek is found in a field where white salt covers the ground like frost. Travelers see no tower there. They see a dark circle, twelve narrow stones, and a cloud that remains directly overhead regardless of the wind.

The circle reflects a staircase climbing toward an open sky. Its reflection contains no traveler until someone steps between the stones. Then a figure appears upon the stair, already several steps ahead of whoever has entered the field.

The villagers call the circle the summit. They call the cloud the root. Their oldest songs describe a tower that grew downward after its keeper mistook the stars for seeds.

## The Farmer of White Embers

A farmer tends the field with a rake made of pale bone. His furrows remain visible through the salt but vanish whenever rain approaches. He carries small white embers in a clay bowl, planting one beside each stone before sunset.

The embers do not burn the soil. They make it transparent. Through the furrows, visitors glimpse rooms arranged around a staircase, their windows facing a sky deep beneath the field.

The farmer explains nothing. When asked what he grows, he touches the bowl and looks toward the cloud. Sometimes a long scaled shape can be seen moving there, its wings spread around a cluster of dark roots.

After those appearances, the farmer's shadow has horns. He waits until the horns disappear before returning to the village.

## The Stairwalker Who Stayed Ahead

A traveler entered the circle hoping to catch the figure in its reflection. She took one step. The figure took none, but the distance between them remained unchanged.

She continued until the reflected staircase passed through the circle's edge and appeared beneath the field. Her feet still stood upon salt. Below them, the figure turned toward a window containing the traveler herself, watching from inside a small room.

The traveler called her own name. The window closed. Far above, the cloud released a single dark root, which hung over the field without touching it.

She left before the farmer returned. At home, she found salt upon her windowsill and a clay bowl beneath her chair. The bowl contained an ember shaped like the figure's raised hand.

## The Twelve Stones and Their Silence

Each stone bears a shallow hollow. The hollows contain no inscriptions, yet observers sometimes recognize words there after waking from dreams of the tower.

The first stone recalls a doorway. The second recalls a room without a floor. The third recalls a creature sleeping with its head among roots. The remaining stones refuse consistent descriptions, and the twelfth changes whenever someone tries to repeat the first.

A village scribe arranged the accounts in a circle. His pages began showing a staircase between their lines. At its upper end stood a farmer carrying a bowl. At its lower end, a cloud moved beneath a field of salt.

The scribe turned the pages upside down. The staircase remained in place. The farmer's bowl became empty, and a small ember appeared on the scribe's desk.

## The Words around the Root

**Vor — yn — thazek — aelun.**

These sounds are heard when the dark root descends far enough to cast a shadow between the stones. They resemble words spoken through a wall filled with rain.

The farmer once held his bowl beneath the root. A drop fell into it, though the cloud contained no rain. Inside the drop, the reflected staircase acquired an additional step.

A scholar tried to measure that step. His ruler described the length of a wing folded beneath the field. He tried again, and the measurement became the distance between two windows in his childhood home.

He left the ruler beside the twelfth stone. Its shadow rose slowly toward the cloud, becoming a thin branch before it vanished among the roots.

## The Farmer's Three Replies

> Why does the tower grow beneath the field?
>
> Because the seeds remembered another sky.
>
> Who walks ahead upon the reflected stair?
>
> The traveler whose window has not opened.
>
> What ripens where the white embers are planted?
>
> The room that will return the root to its cloud.

The scribe preserved these replies in a clay tablet. During winter, the tablet grows warm and shows the reflection of an open window. During summer, it becomes cold enough to gather salt upon its edges.

Nobody can agree which season is visible through the reflected window. The farmer has never looked at the tablet, though its clay bears the same mark as his bowl.

## The Harvest beneath the Cloud

The field remains white. Its circle continues reflecting a staircase, and its twelve stones keep their shallow hollows turned toward the cloud.

The farmer plants the embers before every sunset. Visitors occasionally find a new furrow shaped like a wing, but the salt covers it before the villagers can decide where its owner might have rested.

Vorynthazek grows toward a sky hidden beneath its own summit. Far overhead, a root waits inside a cloud, while somewhere below the field a window opens upon a traveler who has not yet entered the circle.
